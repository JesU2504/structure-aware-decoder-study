# QEC/FTQC prior-art audit: selective pre-execution decoder profiling

**16 November 2026, Asia/Tokyo. Separate AI reviewer; not external peer review.** This is a focused primary-source novelty audit, independently reconsidering the proposed direction. I collected no decoder timings, fitted no models, and used no additional agents. Earlier reports and frozen artifacts are preserved.

## Verdict

**The narrow claim is plausible but unverified; novelty is not established.** In the methods examined, I did not identify the complete proposed workflow: an offline planner uses a cheap frozen circuit/DEM cost model to choose which candidate tasks receive additional physical-syndrome CPU timing samples before service-budget admission. That bounded observation is not evidence that no such prior work exists.

Several broader novelty claims are **contradicted** by close QEC/FTQC work. Decoder-aware feasibility, calibrated stochastic latency, timing/accuracy-based decoder choice, admission control, selective expensive decoding, and adaptive allocation of QEC simulation effort are already studied. The strongest newly identified adjacent overlap is **DART-Q**, which explicitly addresses admission, tails, overload, and capacity. Its methods differ materially from the proposed offline measurement-selection problem.

Consequently, a defensible prospective contribution would be an empirically validated **measurement-allocation and admission result in a specified QEC domain**, potentially using an established method. A new ML algorithm is not required. Neither the generic principle of selectively buying information nor the current deadband rule should be presented as intrinsically novel.

## Exact comparison boundary

The proposed inputs are a candidate circuit/DEM, H/L, known noise priors, hardware/decoder configuration, and a service budget. The optional action is to purchase additional **pre-execution physical-syndrome timing observations of the same decoder task**. The final decision admits, rejects, or abstains on a per-call timing requirement while separately respecting logical quality. The outputs of interest are profiling cost, useful feasible admissions, timing-miss risk, and quality.

This differs from selecting a decoder for an already observed hard syndrome, detecting physical noise drift during computation, buying samples to estimate logical failure probability, or dropping an arriving job because a live queue is full. These distinctions concern inputs, timing, and decisions; changing labels alone would not establish novelty.

I searched combinations of decoder/runtime prediction, cost models, profiling, adaptive/selective measurement, admission, uncertainty, calibration, and compilation; followed the closest QEC/FTQC sources; and inspected method sections rather than relying on titles. The matrix includes both strong overlaps and adjacent work that constrains the claim. It is not an exhaustive systematic review or a patent clearance.

One additional current check was [FusionQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13111), §§4.3–4.4 and 5.1. It uses the known DEM plus arriving detector outcomes to grow/merge local decoding problems, overlaps solves with acquisition, and races a monolithic fallback. Its actual timing evaluation concerns that online execution strategy. It does not establish the proposed offline selective-profiling workflow; equally, online cluster scheduling and latency reduction cannot be claimed as this project's contribution.

I also inspected ScaLERQEC's current [uniformized-profiling documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yezhuoyang/ScaLERQEC/main/docs/uniformized_profiling.md), separately from the paper's S-curve method. It documents importance-weighted logical-failure estimation across noise targets, finite-grid uncertainty bounds, checkpoint stopping, and cooperative resource budgets. Its measured outcome is logical failure, not decoder latency; its choice is how long to sample that estimator, not which candidate runtime profile to buy before admission. This is relevant precedent for budgeted, uncertainty-aware QEC testing, **not an identified exact collision**. The mutable project documentation should not be conflated with the paper's published method or with a verified service-time guarantee.

## What the nearest methods actually rule out

**DART-Q is an important qualification, not an exact collision.** Its live-queue admission and regime-analysis service models challenge generic admission novelty. They do not demonstrate selective acquisition of candidate timing data. This distinction follows from the actual admission rule and stated service-model scope. [DART-Q, §§III-B, V-B/E, VI-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09142).

PACE is closer in *decision location*: its cost model determines candidate schedule feasibility. Delfosse et al. are closer in *evidence*: actual runtime distributions and logical outcomes inform offline choices. ScaLER is closer in *measurement allocation*: a fitted model directs expensive QEC sampling. A reviewer could reasonably view the proposal as combining already known elements. That combination can still contribute new empirical knowledge or a useful domain adaptation, but the burden is to show its effect rather than advertise the components as new.

I found no inspected learned-runtime paper that demonstrates the exact proposed acquisition/admission loop. This does not establish a vacant research area. In particular, “calibration,” “profiling,” “adaptive,” and “admission” retrieve many different meanings; failure to find an identical title is weak evidence. The open-source/API and recent-preprint landscape can also contain implementations not fully described in the nearest papers.

## Remaining claim and publication-relevant boundary

A potentially distinct claim is:

> For a stated family of circuit-derived tasks and a pinned CPU decoder, selecting pre-execution physical-syndrome timing measurements using inexpensive task information reduces total characterization cost at comparable useful admissions and calibrated service-miss risk, while maintaining a separately stated logical-quality requirement.

This is **plausible but unverified**, and deliberately does not require structural features or a new learning algorithm. It would be an empirical/domain-adaptation result if established. A simple model succeeding is compatible with this claim.

Three qualifications materially narrow it:

- **Measurement choice must add value.** Uniform profiling and a simple model-filter-then-profile-every-proposed-admission policy are mandatory controls. If that latter policy matches the more elaborate gate, the stronger selective-acquisition claim is weak; the simpler policy might still support a useful scoped result.
- **Calibration must cover the policy being claimed.** A historical multiplier is not a prediction interval or noise-transfer guarantee. Unprofiled model acceptance needs its own defensible calibration/assumptions; alternatively, restrict acceptance to profiled/certified cases. “No confident false admissions in development” is neither a risk bound nor a substitute for unresolved-case accounting.
- **Admission is more than a timing label.** The quality screen, feasible rejection cost, actual profiling/setup/inference cost, hardware context, and operating-domain restrictions belong in the claim. The existing nineteen configurations/seven parent groups are development evidence; they cannot establish prospective generalization or reusable calibrated risk.

A raw reduction in profiling calls, a better runtime regressor, or a favorable budget chosen after inspection would therefore leave novelty and usefulness **weak**. A demonstrated cost/yield/risk frontier on independently held-out programs would make the narrower transfer claim more credible. The present audit establishes neither that result nor that nobody has already achieved it.

**Disposition:** broad method novelty is contradicted or weak; the exact QEC-specific selective-measurement/admission claim remains **plausible-but-unverified**. Proceeding to a bounded feasibility audit is reasonable, but presenting the direction as novel, safe, or publication-ready is premature. This conclusion is independent of whether a simple baseline or a richer model ultimately supplies the useful gate.
